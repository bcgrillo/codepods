TITLE: Animal Farm Meets AI: The Glorious, Ultimate Final Mill

Squealer appeared on screen with his radiant smile and a voice tuned like a commercial. The stage of his channel flashed with LED lights in the colors of X Farm, while an animated logo —a corn cob with crossed leaves— spun nonstop.

[FIGURE]
“Welcome, friends and followers of The Squealer Report,” he shouted with almost squeaky enthusiasm. “Today we have a guest who needs no introduction: the visionary leader, the architect of our future, CEO of X Farm, and almighty president of Animal Farm, the incomparable Napoleon.”

As the number of animals connected quickly increased, the virtual audience exploded in cheers, corn emojis, and endless auto-messages: Great Napoleon! The future is now!

Napoleon entered with a firm step, wearing a minimalist black T-shirt, limited edition white sneakers, and his tail tied back with a discreet designer hair tie. He had the posture and confident air of a true Silic-Corn Valley technocrat.

“Friends,” he began without waiting for a question, “I have come to present the Final Mill. It is not just a data center, it is the peak of centuries of animal effort. Inside it will be born AnimAGI-1, the first animal general intelligence. With it, our robots will finally free every hoof, claw, and wing from work. The age of scarcity is about to end.”

The chat exploded: Technological miracle! Blessed Napoleon!

And it was not just exaggeration, many crows already revered him like a messianic figure. Most animals hardly remembered that these promises sounded very similar to those of the first windmill, when electricity was supposed to free them from a life of effort and sacrifice. The wealth they saw in the pigs’ luxurious lives they took as their own, convinced that abundance would reach them sooner or later. Then came other mills: electronics, computers, internet, robots… Each one with the same promise: a more comfortable life, shorter workdays, more free time, abundance for all. If it hadn’t come yet, it must be very close. And besides, none of those mills had been as grand as this one. The Final Mill promised to be different and irreversible.

Squealer almost choked with excitement:

“What a wonder, what a miracle! The mill that will take Animal Farm to technological supremacy once and for all. Tell us, dear Napoleon, when will that glorious day arrive?”

“Very soon,” Napoleon answered solemnly, “almost certainly in six months, maybe a year. But beware: no one should lose focus. You all know I am myself an example of sacrifice: I have always defended the 100-hour work week. Work will end, yes, but only if we work harder than ever to make it happen.”

Among the thousands watching live there was also a donkey, who lived in a broken cart parked for months in the X Farm employees’ lot. He nodded every time Napoleon spoke of sacrifice, repeating to himself that he was a genius for choosing “to live in transport” instead of in a stable. He said this because it was a free and bold decision that allowed him to save most of his rations, not because it was the only option left after being evicted from the shelter of his late father Benjamin. The young donkey joined the other virtual applause filling the screen: Napoleon genius! Example of sacrifice!

Squealer, always ready with the narrative, coughed with fake indignation:

“Sadly, not all share this shining vision. Snowball, for example, has appeared again on his channel, daring to criticize these plans, saying our data centers use ‘too much water and electricity’”, he forced a ridiculous voice, “and even worse, he suggested raising taxes on the big pigs!”

A murmur of disapproval spread through the chat.

Napoleon growled in contempt:

“Snowball is nothing but a woke charlatan, enemy of animal progress. He talks about ecology, redistribution, stopping the talented with taxes. That pig wants to turn off the light of the future with a bucket of ideological dung! It’s just another of his crazy ideas, like that so-called universal feed income, or taxing animaloid robots. Nonsense! Anything but work.”

The chat roared: Snowball traitor! Snowball communist!

“Talent,” Napoleon continued, “must never ask permission to shine. Taxes should always be minimal, they are chains on our legs, obstacles against freedom. Only the free market, without ties, can guarantee prosperity.”

Squealer nodded strongly, as if the words were a revelation from the sky.

“Exactly, exactly,” he clapped, “and let’s remember, dear viewers, what the Commandment of the Free Market says: ‘The market regulates itself… as long as the other farms are friends.’”

The virtual applause multiplied. Nobody mentioned that, in practice, Animal Farm had tariffs against all neighbor farms. Or that many times the pigs justified other taxes for “strategic subsidies” or for defense expenses of Animal Farm.

In the middle of the interview, Squealer frowned with fake drama, as if revealing a conspiracy:

“Some animals invited by Snowball, blinded by prejudice, have started complaining: they say their songs, their drawings in the dirt, their moos, in short, their work and creations are being used without permission to train the new animal intelligence! Some even dare to call it theft. What nerve! How can we call theft what is really a contribution to Animal Farm’s progress?”

Napoleon, smiling with superiority, leaned toward the camera:

“Exactly. It’s not theft, but contribution. Every bleat, grunt, and chirp that AnimAGI-1 absorbs is part of a collective offering to the future. The real theft would be letting these treasures waste away in forgotten barns. Animals should feel proud their work is part of the Final Mill. The progress of Animal Farm cannot and must not ask permission.”

The chat exploded in applause and praise: Great Napoleon! All for progress!

Squealer then raised another hot topic:

“And don’t think Snowball stopped at questioning our glorious data centers. No, friends! He also shouted about the price of shelters. As if we didn’t already have too many regulations punishing pig investors. And worse: he dared to mention Napoleon directly, asking if work disappears thanks to automation as X Farm promises, how will a humble animal buy, not just a shed, but even its daily food?”

Napoleon smiled sarcastically and settled into his chair:

“Buy, buy, buy… That’s because they don’t know the future,” he said, with a pompous voice. “As we predicted in Davos: ‘In the future you’ll own nothing. And you’ll be happy…’”

Squealer instantly joined, finishing the phrase in perfect duet:

“‘…and your feed subscription will be delivered by a Farmazon drone!’”

Both pigs laughed loudly, and the audience copied them with laughing emojis, drones, and grain sacks. Nobody asked how those subscriptions would be paid if one day incomes disappeared.

Squealer, with a mocking smile, raised a hologram showing the face of Clover, now one of the most known voices of new unionism on Animal Farm.

“Look, dear viewers,” he said with fake worry. “Clover has spent weeks, from her new podcast, giving false and alarmist speeches, saying big corporations want to limit the associations of working animals. As if that was not just another invention to stop progress!”

Napoleon snorted in disdain and waved his hoof, scaring off the fly that had followed him since the start.

“Clover? That mare with her constant whining. She talks about unions and equality, but she forgets it was her comrade Boxer who died working to the end for progress, without complaint, like many other male animals. She just climbed onto that feminist, resentful, violent rhetoric. The stampedes organized lately by those unionists could be called animal terrorism.”

The chat reacted with boos, insults, and angry emojis against Clover.

Squealer reinforced the blow:

“Let’s not forget, dear friends, what one of our sacred commandments says: freedom above all… but never to stop progress.”

Napoleon added solemnly, lifting his hoof like he was reciting a universal truth, while Squealer watched him with open mouth in theatrical pose:

“And also: no freedom to live on Animal Farm if you were born on another farm! Let’s not forget: most of these agitators came here illegally and must be deported immediately.”

The virtual audience went wild. Between messages like Beat up those unionists!” and “Out with immigrant animals!”, one message stood out, written in capital letters by an excited colt:

“GREETINGS FROM ARGENTINA ¡VIVA LA LIBERTAD ANIMAL, CARAJO!”

Animals repeated and supported these slogans without noticing any contradiction.

Meanwhile, on screen, they showed ads of the new robot dogs from Bestial Dynamics, efficiently crushing a union protest in Farmazon warehouses.

Later, in the segment of the Commandments of Free Animals, Squealer displayed the shining list:

- Taxes will always be minimal → crossed out, replaced by Taxes will be as needed for strategic subsidies and our security.
- The market regulates itself → crossed out, replaced by The market regulates itself as long as the other farms are friends.
- Private property is sacred → crossed out, replaced by Subscription is sacred and intellectual property must serve progress.
- Freedom above all → crossed out, replaced by Freedom ends where the fence begins.The sheep in the chat repeated it together, like a religious chant:“Freedom ends where the fence begins!”

To close the program, Squealer showered Napoleon with praise, and not only him: he extended his compliments to the whole elite of the big pigs, those titans who —according to him— had reached the top only thanks to their effort and sacrifice.

“Because thanks to freedom and the market,” he said seriously, “any animal can achieve it. It doesn’t matter if one is born in a ruined stall or inherits a fortune of thousands of feed sacks: opportunities are there, for everyone.”

Napoleon, with a satisfied smile, nodded slowly.

“That’s right,” he declared. “That is what meritocracy means, our fifth and last commandment: equal opportunities for all animals… only that”, he paused, raising his eyebrow, “obviously some opportunities will always be more equal than others.”

The virtual herd exploded in applause and cheers, convinced they had listened to eternal wisdom.

To end the interview, Squealer, with firm voice, asked the last question:

“Napoleon, one last question. Tell us, what will be the next challenge for animals once we reach supremacy with the Final Mill?”

Napoleon raised his hoof, pointing to the sky, in an uncomfortable and strangely familiar gesture. Looking upward, he pronounced his final words:

“The limit is the stars, Squealer. We must take Animal Farm to Mars.”

While the stage turned dark with digital ovations, on the night shift of a poor farm on the other side of the world, a poor duck worker watched the live show on his old phone resting on a box.

His wings shook with tiredness, but he kept sewing cap after cap on a mountain of cheap hats. On the one he was holding, white letters were embroidered on green:

“Make Animal Farm Great Again”

The duck, with glassy eyes, thought the only reason his farm did not reach the excellence of Animal Farm was the lack of brilliant minds like Napoleon’s. If only they had a single visionary pig born in the incubators of Sili-Corn Valley, maybe they too could dream of a Final Mill, feed drones, and the promise of a happy future.

He kept sewing in silence, convinced his sacrifice was the door to a better tomorrow.

The next day, his factory was closed as part of Animal Farm’s new relocation policy.

### Author’s Note
Hello! I am Bruno Grillo, I work in technology, I enjoy programming, and I truly believe in the huge potential of artificial intelligence to automate processes and improve people’s lives.

Thanks for reading this far!

I don’t write this story against innovation or technology. But I keep wondering if we are building the right strategy so that all of us can benefit from what we are creating.

In Animal Farm, Orwell showed how a project born with good intentions ended up corrupted for the benefit of a few. Today, in the developed world, we should ask ourselves what ensures that unstoppable automation will not leave millions behind, as has already happened before.

In the end, it is us — together — who are building the future. And, at least in theory, we can also decide how to do it and how to share it.
